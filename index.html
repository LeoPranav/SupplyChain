Brief write-up

The core D2C insight is that logistics losses usually come from silent unit economics, not headline freight rates. Most brands negotiate good base pricing but leak margin in the exceptions: incorrect zone assignment, slab escalations from rounded weights, and surcharges that should be conditional but are treated as default. The design prioritizes these high-frequency leakage points first, so users can see where variance accumulates across billing cycles.

Building for finance operations meant reducing review friction. Instead of presenting extraction output alone, the interface mirrors payout decisions: billed amount, expected amount, variance reason, and actionability. This improves adoption because operations and finance can align quickly on recoveries and dispute packets.

The system also separates provider-specific invoice noise from a normalized audit model. That matters because invoice layouts differ, but contractual logic does not. By structuring checks around business rules (weight slabs, zone tables, surcharge entitlements), the approach remains extensible across Delhivery, Blue Dart, Ecom Express, and future partners without redesigning core UX flows.
